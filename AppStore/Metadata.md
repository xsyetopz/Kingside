## Description

Kingside brings the complete game to Apple TV with an interface designed specifically for the television.

Play bots or a friend using only the Siri Remote, or connect a compatible Bluetooth game controller. Navigate the board directionally, select a piece, inspect its legal moves, and watch each move slide into place.

Features:

- Play Bots: original bot personalities from 250 to 2200 Elo
- Play a Friend: pass-and-play on one Apple TV with bullet, blitz, rapid, or untimed clocks
- Rated puzzles, Puzzle Rush (3 Minutes, 5 Minutes, Survival), and a Daily Puzzle
- Game Review with accuracy, move classifications from brilliant to blunder, an evaluation graph, and best-line preview
- Archive of your finished games
- Complete chess rules, including castling, en passant, promotion, and draws by repetition, fifty moves, and insufficient material
- Board themes, classic piece set, and gameplay options such as legal-move dots, coordinates, and auto-promotion
- Resign, draw offer, takeback, hint, and board flip
- Automatic restoration of unfinished games
- Siri Remote and Bluetooth controller support
- Play bots and puzzles offline; optional Lichess sign-in for online games

Kingside has an original interface, icon, and board design; the piece artwork is the BSD-licensed cburnett set (see ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS.md). It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Chess.com.

## App Review notes

No account or login is required. Games, bots, puzzles, and Game Review work offline. Network requests happen only for Game Center sign-in, for the optional Chess.com link (public API, read-only), and for the optional Lichess sign-in and online play. The application is not affiliated with Chess.com.

## App Privacy

Data Not Collected. The developer runs no server and includes no third-party SDKs; data sent to
Lichess or Chess.com goes to the service the user signs in to, which Apple's definition of
"collect" does not cover. The reasoning is in ADR 0002, decision 4
(`docs/architecture/0002-online-services-and-partner-access.md`).
